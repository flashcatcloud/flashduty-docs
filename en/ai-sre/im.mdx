## Overview

Incident response usually happens right inside your IM group — alerts come in, the team discusses in the channel, and someone spins up a war room. AI SRE puts the agent directly into that collaboration chain: **you never have to switch to the console. You can summon it from IM to investigate**, and your teammates can follow every step of its analysis in real time.

AI SRE's IM integration has three trigger modes:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="@Mention Summon" icon="at">
    **@mention AI SRE** in a group chat or DM and describe the problem to start or continue an investigation. It replies **in-thread**, and the conversation context is tied to that IM session.
  </Card>
  <Card title="War Room Auto-Diagnosis" icon="people-group">
    When someone opens an IM war room for an incident, AI SRE runs a preliminary diagnosis and posts the findings to the war room if the account has an enabled **War room created** automation rule that the incident's channel and severity match. No one needs to summon it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Automation Replies in the Group" icon="clock">
    Ask AI SRE in a group to create an ordinary automation rule and it uses **this group** as the run location: when the rule later fires on a schedule, an API call, or an On-call incident, the run **opens a new session** in the original group and posts its conclusion there. Every run is a fresh session and does not carry over the group's earlier conversation; when the integration has thread replies on, each run also opens a new thread whose root message is the run's first real output (progress or conclusion), with no "started" placeholder posted before there is content. The binding and scope are fixed at creation and cannot move to another group: before each run they are re-checked, and if the group's team scope or session has changed the whole run is **skipped**.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The first two modes are started by a person; the third is started by an automation rule you configured. For how to create the rule and how its execution place and scope are fixed, see [Automations](/en/ai-sre/automations#execution-place-where-a-rule-runs).

## Supported IM platforms

AI SRE's IM interaction covers four major platforms. Each platform supports inbound @mentions (webhook), historical message reads, and outbound replies:

| Platform | Group @mention | Direct message (DM) | Processing receipt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slack | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ Emoji (plus a native "is working on it" status) |
| Lark / Feishu | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ Emoji |
| DingTalk | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ Built-in emoji |
| WeCom | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ Placeholder message |

A "processing receipt" is the immediate signal the bot gives once it has your message; how it looks on each platform is described under [Processing Receipt](#processing-receipt).

<Note>
IM interaction requires that you have already connected the corresponding platform's bot in Flashduty (the same IM bot used for alert notifications and collaboration). Complete the bot configuration in Flashduty's IM integration first — only then can AI SRE send and receive messages on that platform.
</Note>

## AI SRE settings in IM integrations

Open the corresponding IM integration under **On-call → Integration Center → Integration List → Instant Messaging** and configure its AI SRE behavior under **Enhanced features**. Both switches below are on by default:

- **Allow AI SRE conversations in IM**: available without enabling War Room. It controls both entry points — **group @mentions** and **direct chats with the bot**; in Slack it also controls the `/fd` command. Turning it off makes group @mentions and direct chats with the bot (and, in Slack, `/fd`) silent. In WeCom's Third-party App mode, only direct chats with the bot are available; group @mentions require Custom App Integration mode.
- **Use thread replies in normal group chats**: available only for Feishu/Lark and Slack. When enabled, each normal-group thread has an independent session; War Rooms still receive replies directly in the group.

<Note>
If AI SRE is not enabled for the account, these switches do not take effect. After AI SRE is enabled, each IM integration follows its own settings.
</Note>

## @Mention summon

In a connected IM group with **Allow AI SRE conversations in IM** on, **@mention the bot** and type your question, for example "@AI SRE check why the payment service's 5xx rate is spiking." The platform webhook forwards the message to AI SRE:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Detect the mention">
    The platform distinguishes between **group @mentions** and **direct messages**. Once mentioned, AI SRE puts a "processing" receipt on that message (see [Processing receipt](#processing-receipt)) and checks whether the message contains a slash command.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bind to a session">
    Multiple @mentions within the same IM chat continue the **same agent session**, preserving context. For a new chat, AI SRE starts with the opening message of that IM thread and any associated incident as context. In a Feishu / Lark group with topic replies on, a topic session opened by a **top-level @** also pulls in the group messages from the **last 10 minutes** (up to that @), which is how an error text or screenshot posted just before the @ reaches the context; replies inside a topic read only that topic.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read context and reply">
    AI SRE reads the thread / chat history to build context, investigates autonomously, and posts its findings **in-thread**. Other people @mentioned in the original message are carried over into the reply, making it easy for multiple parties to collaborate in the group.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
The **reply mode** is configurable (off / first / all), controlling whether AI SRE @mentions the person who asked, and whether it replies **in-thread** or in the main channel. In a busy large group, in-thread replies keep the investigation discussion focused without flooding the channel.
</Tip>

### Attachments and images

AI SRE downloads images, documents and voice messages from the chat and hands them to the agent as files. This covers not only attachments on the message that **@mentions** it but also attachments on **earlier messages in the same thread or chat**: post an error screenshot, then add "@bot what is this?", and the model receives the screenshot.

Per turn, the attachment quota is counted by type. The current message's attachments take quota first, and history fills the rest, newest first:

| Type | Limit per turn |
| --- | --- |
| Images | 5 |
| Documents | 3 |
| Voice messages | 3 |

- **History attachments that don't fit** are marked "not attached" in the prompt, so the model is never promised a file it doesn't have.
- **Rejected:** an attachment over 20 MiB, or a direct link that is not HTTPS.
- **Time budget for history:** history attachments not downloaded within about 5 seconds are treated as not attached, so a turn never waits on an old screenshot.
- **All attachments of the current message fail:** AI SRE does not call the agent and replies with a short failure notice asking you to retry or send a smaller file. A failed history attachment does not stop the turn.

### Processing receipt

Once the bot has your message, it first puts an emoji reaction on **the message you sent**, as an immediate "received, working on it" signal. The emoji is picked at random from a small set for each platform:

| Platform | Emoji |
| --- | --- |
| Slack | eyes, hourglass_flowing_sand |
| Lark / Feishu | Typing, OneSecond |
| DingTalk | Platform built-in emotions: 专注, 忙疯了 |

On Slack this sits alongside a native "is working on it" status, which the bot also shows.

The reaction is removed once the answer is delivered.

WeCom has no emoji API, so its "processing" receipt is a placeholder message, such as a random "On it…". The task checklist card and the final answer then replace it in place.

### How a reply reaches the chat

Once you hand AI SRE an investigation, every message that appears in the group is one of two kinds:

- **Result**: an answer to your concrete question, or an actionable interim finding.
- **Progress**: a one-or-two-sentence update while work is still going on: confirmed progress, what it is waiting on, a real blocker. A progress message is **not the answer**: it does not finish the turn, and the result is posted separately as usual.

When AI SRE hands the work to a background Subagent (or an A2A delegation) and nothing has been sent to the chat yet in that turn, it first posts a short **acknowledgement** saying what it dispatched and that the conclusion will follow when the Subagent reports back, so the group is not left silent until the child finishes. This is a progress message too: it does not end the turn.

This reply style applies only to **human-initiated IM requests**: console sessions still answer with streaming text, and war-room auto-diagnosis is an unattended background turn that also keeps native streaming output.

<Note>
When a reply is too long, AI SRE trims it or publishes the detailed evidence as an artifact and posts only the conclusion and the artifact link (see [Artifacts](/en/ai-sre/artifacts)). This limit does not apply when you explicitly ask for the full report in the chat.
</Note>

## War room auto-diagnosis

Whether AI SRE steps into a war room on its own is decided by the account's **War room created** automation rules, not by a switch on the IM integration. **A matching rule is what makes the diagnosis run**. No one needs to @mention it, but you do need a rule in place:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the war room">
    Create a war room (Lark / DingTalk / WeCom / Slack group) as part of the incident collaboration workflow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Match a war-room-created rule">
    The platform matches the new war room against the account's enabled **War room created** rules: the incident's **channel** must be in the rule's list and the incident's **severity** must match the rule. Both have to hold before a run starts. With no matching rule, no analysis is produced.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Post the findings">
    The run is carried on that war room's IM group (no console session is created). AI SRE investigates with the incident's context and posts its findings back to the war room. Before anyone has started investigating manually, the first-cut analysis is already in the group.
  </Step>
</Steps>

For which fields the trigger card asks for, and how to create the rule, see [Automations · War room created](/en/ai-sre/automations#war-room-created).

<Note>
War room auto-diagnosis is bound to the incident context. When the investigation begins, the platform attaches the `incident_id` to the run, so AI SRE can read the incident details, timeline, and recent changes. For details on how incidents / war rooms interact with A2A, see [Agent · Incident & War Room Integration](/en/ai-sre/agents#incident-and-war-room-integration).
</Note>

## Standing tasks and the monitoring card

Sometimes the agent's turn ends while a **standing process-type task** it started is still running, such as a monitor watcher or a background command. AI SRE then posts a monitoring card to the chat:

```
🔭 Monitoring: <task description> (up to N minutes)
🔭 Monitoring: <task description> (indefinitely)
```

Each standing task gets one line: tasks with a deadline show the remaining minutes (under one minute rounds up to one minute); tasks without a deadline show "indefinitely". The card is re-posted whenever the standing-task list changes.

<Note>
While any standing task is alive, the IM session's root message stays open, and so does the incident view. Later turns that task notifications trigger keep posting into the **same chat**. You don't need to @mention the bot again.
</Note>

When a notification turn has nothing new, AI SRE posts nothing; it speaks only when there is a new conclusion.

### Progress messages during a long investigation

In long investigations, AI SRE posts a progress update or two in the group saying what it is still checking, what is blocking, and what comes next. A progress message is not the answer and doesn't end the turn; the result follows separately. If nothing has changed, or it is waiting for your input, AI SRE may stay quiet.

## Connections and authorization

In the console, a missing credential shows an authorization card (see [Console conversations](/en/ai-sre/sessions#when-authorization-is-required)). **IM and API channels have no cards**, so the agent handles it this way:

| Situation | Behavior on IM / API channels |
| --- | --- |
| Your OAuth authorization is missing | The agent posts the authorize link; open it in a **desktop browser**, complete authorization, then reply **"已授权"** (or `authorized` in an English session) to continue. A successful authorization does not resume the task by itself |
| Your secret is missing | The agent asks you to open **this same session in the web console** to enter it; it never asks you to paste a secret into the chat |
| The account has not connected the required integration | The agent posts the console link to connect it; connect, then reply **"继续"** (or `continue`) |
| A shared server's own credentials (environment variables / headers) | Not any of the above: fill them in under **Customize → Plugins → MCP** (that server), then tell the agent to retry |

On the automation channel, nobody is watching and no reply will arrive. The agent doesn't wait or ask questions. It **records the missing connection or authorization as a blocker** in its final report and delivers what the evidence already supports.

## In-session switch commands

IM sessions have no picker UI like the console. Instead, two slash commands let you **switch bindings mid-conversation**. Console sessions don't support these commands, because their environment and team bindings are fixed at creation.

### /env — Switch Runner environment

Send `/env <target>` to the bot in a group or DM to rebind the current IM session to a different execution environment. The conversation history and context are preserved in full. After the switch, you can't access the previous environment's working directory. Files created or changed there during the conversation are gone. AI SRE re-stages skill and knowledge files in the new environment on demand.

`<target>` takes three forms:

| Form | Switches to |
| --- | --- |
| `/env <runner-name>` | The named online BYOC runner |
| `/env sandbox` | The default cloud sandbox |
| `/env sandbox <template-name>` | A named cloud sandbox template (a shared template, or one belonging to a team you are in) |

Send `/env` with no argument and the bot lists every target currently available: shared environments, your teams' environments, and the cloud sandbox (the default sandbox plus any named templates). If a named sandbox template is not found, the bot replies "No cloud sandbox template named "x". Use /env sandbox for the default sandbox." along with the list of available templates.

<Note>
The switch takes effect after the current turn completes and before the next message is processed — it does not interrupt a tool call that is already running. If the target runner is offline or not found, the command returns an error immediately and the binding remains unchanged.
</Note>

<Note>
**On-premises deployments have no cloud sandbox.** On-premises installs have no cloud sandbox infrastructure, so `/env` shows no sandbox option or entries. You can only switch between BYOC runners and Auto.
</Note>

### /scope — Switch team scope

Send `/scope <team-name>` (or `/scope personal` to return to personal scope) to rebind the current IM session to a different team without ending the conversation. Team binding is a **usage operation**: any account member can bind a session to any team in the account. This fits war rooms, where responders often aren't members of the incident's team.

After the switch, AI SRE:

1. Refreshes the memory snapshot for the new team scope (memories from the previous team scope no longer apply to this session).
2. Queues the new team's knowledge bundle to be mounted and injected into context when the next message is processed.
3. Re-checks whether the session's pinned execution environment is still available. If the pin is a BYOC runner or cloud sandbox template that belongs **exclusively to another team**, the bot resets the environment to Auto and replies ""x" does not belong to the current team. The runtime environment has been reset to Auto." Shared and account-level environments are unaffected. If the check is inconclusive, for example because the template list can't be fetched, the pin stays as is.

<Note>
Knowledge bundles already mounted into this session are not removed — mounts are conversation-scoped, so switching back to a previously-mounted team will not duplicate the mount reminder.
</Note>

## /feedback — Rate an AI SRE reply

Once an AI SRE reply lands in a group chat, anyone in the group can rate it — not only whoever started the investigation. Send `/feedback` to the bot to rate or comment on the reply you are looking at:

- **Entry and resolution:** `/feedback` applies to the reply you are looking at, even if you never took part in that conversation.
- **Eligibility:** **not limited to session members** — any account member who can use AI SRE may rate (read-only group-chat readers included).
- **How to rate:** choose `like` / `dislike` / `none`. A comment-only submission (no rating) is allowed and does not wipe an earlier rating you gave, while an explicit `none` clears the existing rating.
- **Several raters:** when several people rate the same reply, each rating is kept and none overwrites another.
- **Console side:** the console conversation header also offers thumbs-up feedback on replies (see [Feedback](/en/ai-sre/sessions#session-feedback)), sharing the same rating system as IM `/feedback`.

## Relationship to console sessions

Whether you start a session from IM or the console, it's **the same AI SRE**. It handles messages one at a time and streams output. It compacts context automatically in long conversations and supports optional team and environment bindings. The only difference is the entry point —

- **Console**: ask questions one at a time in the **Chat** workspace and view the full tool-call and sub-session panels. Environment and team bindings are fixed at creation and cannot be changed.
- **IM**: @mention it in the group where your team already works; findings are posted back to the thread. Use `/env` and `/scope` to switch bindings mid-conversation. Great for grabbing a quick analysis at the incident scene, then diving deeper in the console.

For more about sessions, see [Conversations](/en/ai-sre/sessions).
