## Introduction

---

Atlassian has given Opsgenie a definite end: **new sales stopped on June 4, 2025**; existing customers can keep using it until **support ends on April 5, 2027**, after which Opsgenie access shuts off and customer data that has not been moved out will be deleted (see the [Atlassian Opsgenie Licensing FAQ](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/opsgenie)).

<Note>
For teams still running on Opsgenie, the real question is not "should we keep using Opsgenie" — it is **where your schedules and escalation policies live next**.
</Note>

This page compares the two main paths:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Stay inside Atlassian" icon="building">
    Migrate into **Jira Service Management (JSM)** or Compass with Atlassian's official in-app tool — ticketing and alerting from one vendor, with the lowest migration friction
  </Card>
  <Card title="Move to an independent on-call platform" icon="route">
    Flashduty as the example: schedules, escalation, and incident collaboration run independently of JSM packaging, licenses are billed per active responder, with native Feishu/DingTalk/WeCom support
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
The feature and pricing information on this page is current **as of September 2026**; refer to each vendor's official website for the latest updates.
</Note>

## Sunset Timeline and the Official Migration Path

---

### Key Dates

| Date | Event | Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **2025-06-04** | Opsgenie end of sale | No new purchases; plan upgrades/downgrades closed for existing customers; renewals allowed only if the subscription term ends before the end-of-support date |
| **2027-04-05** | Opsgenie end of support | Service access shuts off; customer data not moved into Atlassian's target products is deleted |

### Atlassian's Official Migration Path

Atlassian steers teams toward Jira Service Management (incident management) or Compass (alerting/on-call) through an **in-app migration tool**. Key constraints:

- Migration must be initiated by an **Opsgenie Owner** (who typically also needs site-admin rights);
- The migration date must be scheduled **at least 7 days in advance**;
- After a completed JSM migration, Opsgenie remains available for a **120-day** review period before being permanently shut down;
- Most configuration syncs automatically, but **destination-plan differences and some configuration require manual follow-up** — for example, alert actions, incident rules, Responder roles, and Stakeholder filters do not migrate automatically and must be reviewed and rebuilt in the destination product.

For organizations already standardized on Jira, Confluence, and JSM, this is the lowest-friction path. Teams that want their on-call practice to run independently should evaluate a dedicated on-call platform.

## Product Feature Comparison

---

### On-call

On-call solves "getting the alert to the right person": reduce noise first, then notify according to scheduling and escalation policies.

#### Schedule Management

| Feature | Requirements | Comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rotation Cycle** | Rotate by day, week, or custom period | ✅ Both support |
| **Time Restriction** | Restrict on-call hours within the rotation cycle | ✅ Both support |
| **Temporary Override** | Set temporary schedule overrides | ✅ Both support |
| **On-call Roles** | Set primary/backup and other roles | ✅ **Flashduty exclusive** |
| **Multi-person On-call** | Multiple people on-call at once, rotating together | ✅ **Flashduty exclusive** |
| **Fair Rotation** | Avoid the same person always being on-call at a specific time | ✅ **Flashduty exclusive** (solves the problem where, in a 7-person daily rotation, one person always ends up on-call every Sunday) |
| **Rotation Notifications** | Advance notice and scheduled notices for rotations | ✅ Both support; Flashduty supports more notification methods |

#### Dispatch & Escalation

| Capability | Details | Comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Policy Assignment](/en/on-call/channel/escalation-rule)** | Assign to a schedule, team, or individual, with configurable notification methods | ✅ Both support; Flashduty additionally supports assigning to a team and setting notification methods per policy |
| **Auto Escalation** | Automatically escalate unresolved incidents on timeout, plus manual escalation | ✅ Both support |
| **[Dynamic Assignment](/en/on-call/advanced/dynamic-notifications)** | Dynamically replace or **append** assignees, teams, and group chats based on labels (combinable with CMDB/CSV mapping data to auto-generate assignment targets) | ✅ **Flashduty exclusive**, greatly reducing the cost of system integration and configuration maintenance |

#### Notification Channels

| Method | Notes | Comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Voice/SMS/Email** | Phone call, SMS, and email notifications | ✅ Both support; Flashduty provides a **fixed caller ID** that can sync to phone contact allowlists |
| **China IM** | Collaboration in Feishu, DingTalk, and WeCom | ✅ **Flashduty exclusive**: app integrations **including on-premises versions of Feishu/Lark and DingTalk**; Opsgenie requires bridging or webhook workarounds |
| **International IM** | Slack, Microsoft Teams, and similar | ✅ Both support; Flashduty additionally supports Zoom and Telegram bots |
| **Mobile** | Mobile app | ✅ Both support; Flashduty's iOS app uses the Apple Critical Alerts protocol to **break through Do Not Disturb mode**, and its Android app independently adapts to system-level push from mainstream Chinese device makers |
| **[Custom Templates](/en/on-call/configuration/templates)** | Fully customizable notification templates across all channels, **including voice notification templates**, with historical-incident preview debugging | Flashduty supports rich template syntax; Opsgenie offers limited room for notification content customization |

#### Alert Noise Reduction

| Capability | Description | Comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Alert Grouping** | Group similar alerts to reduce alert fatigue | ✅ Both support (Opsgenie via deduplication and Alert Policies; Flashduty via rule-based and [intelligent alert grouping](/en/on-call/channel/noise-reduction)) |
| **[Incident Inhibition](/en/on-call/channel/noise-reduction)** | Automatically inhibit related pod incidents when the host fails | ✅ **Flashduty exclusive** |
| **[Storm Warning](/en/on-call/channel/noise-reduction)** | Re-notify with escalated urgency when the number of grouped alerts in an incident gets too high, with multi-tier thresholds | ✅ **Flashduty exclusive** |
| **Flapping Convergence** | Moderately reduce notifications when the same incident triggers and recovers repeatedly | ✅ **Flashduty exclusive** |
| **Delayed Notification** | Delay the notification window to filter out incidents that auto-recover immediately | ✅ Both support |

### Response

Response solves "closing the loop quickly once the right person is found": a single timeline, a single war room—handling, collaboration, and postmortems that never lose context.

| Feature | Description | Comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Basic Operations** | Create, acknowledge, close, comment on, snooze, merge, and bulk-assign incidents | ✅ Both support |
| **Incident Management Home** | A dedicated product home for the incident lifecycle | Flashduty ships the full incident lifecycle built in; Opsgenie's Incident Command Center has been deprecated by Atlassian, with incident capabilities folded into JSM |
| **[War Room](/en/on-call/advanced/war-room)** | One-click creation of a Feishu/Lark, DingTalk, WeCom, or Slack group chat, automatically inviting responders, with a pinned incident card that updates in real time and in-chat actions syncing back to the platform | ✅ **Flashduty exclusive** |
| **[Postmortems](/en/on-call/post-mortem/what-is-post-mortem)** | Full postmortem reports: timeline capture, collaborative editing, permission controls, with [AI-generated postmortems](/en/on-call/post-mortem/ai-generate-post-mortem) | ✅ **Flashduty exclusive**; Opsgenie has no equivalent (within Atlassian, postmortems are assembled manually across JSM/Confluence) |
| **Custom Actions** | Add custom actions (e.g. trigger a host restart), with a confirmation step before execution | ✅ Both support (Opsgenie's Alert Actions do not move automatically in the official migration) |

## Pricing Comparison

---

| Dimension | Flashduty | Opsgenie / JSM Path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Billing Model** | License fee + excess communication fees | Per-user seats; after migrating to JSM, billed by the JSM plan |
| **Edition Pricing** | **Professional ¥199/user/month**, including intelligent grouping, AI summaries, and AI postmortems; AI SRE is free during the public beta | Opsgenie historical pricing (1–25 user band, annual): Essentials about \$9.45, Standard about \$19.95, Enterprise about \$31.90 per user/month; JSM has separate Free/Standard/Premium/Enterprise plans |
| **Billing Target** | ✅ **Only active users are charged** — only members who view and handle incidents need a License; notification recipients are free | Responders count as paid seats; Stakeholders (notify-only) are free on Enterprise per the licensing FAQ — evaluate per plan |
| **Status Page Subscriptions** | ✅ Free, no account required | Requires a separately purchased Atlassian Statuspage plan |
| **Free Trial** | Professional 14 days free, extendable on request | No longer sold — no new trials |

### Billing Model Difference Example

Take a 100-person engineering team where 15 people routinely handle incidents:

| Cost Item | Flashduty Professional | Opsgenie Standard (historical) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seats/Licenses required | **15** Licenses | Counted as Responder seats — assume 15 |
| Monthly cost | 15 × ¥199 = **¥2,985/month** | 15 × \$19.95 ≈ **\$299/month** |
| Notification coverage | **100 people** (everyone can receive notifications, free) | Notify-only audiences beyond Responders require checking Stakeholder seat rules per plan |

<Tip>
Opsgenie's seat price is modest, but it is a sunset product — the meaningful cost comparison is between "Flashduty vs your migration destination." If the destination is JSM, include the JSM plan price, an Atlassian Statuspage subscription, and potential seat expansion in the total cost.
</Tip>

## Migration Path Comparison

---

### Path One: Migrate into Jira Service Management (Stay in Atlassian)

Best for organizations already standardized on the Atlassian suite. The official in-app tool syncs most configuration into the selected JSM plan, but note:

- Automation configuration such as alert actions and incident rules **does not migrate automatically** and must be rebuilt with Jira Automation;
- Fields like Responder roles and Stakeholder filters need post-migration review;
- Chat integrations must be reconfigured with new authentication;
- The migration date must be scheduled at least 7 days ahead, with a 120-day review period after completion.

### Path Two: Cut Over to Flashduty (Independent On-call)

Best for teams that want their on-call practice to run independently of JSM packaging. Please be aware: **there is no one-click importer from Opsgenie to Flashduty** — Flashduty specialists help you plan the cutover, and your team deliberately rebuilds schedules, escalation policies, services, routing, and integrations. In return, your alerting platform no longer depends on Atlassian packaging or seat-based billing.

### Recommended Migration Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Audit your existing configuration">
    Inventory schedules and rotation rules, escalation policies, integration endpoints, alert routing rules, and notification templates
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rebuild the core path in the destination">
    Connect one real alert source first; configure schedules and escalation policies; run SMS, phone, IM, escalation, and closure end to end
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run in parallel and verify">
    Keep the old and new response paths running side by side for a while; test acknowledgement and escalation with real responders
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cut over and decommission">
    Once the workflow is proven, agree on a cutover window; export the data you need to keep before the end-of-support date, when unmigrated data will be deleted
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
Whichever path you choose, **complete the migration and export the data you need before April 5, 2027** — unmigrated data will be deleted after the sunset date.
</Warning>

## How to Choose

---

  <Card title="Choose Flashduty if" icon="star">

## Summary

---

Opsgenie was an excellent alerting and on-call product, but its lifecycle is now on a countdown. For existing users, April 5, 2027 is a firm planning boundary: **migration is not a question of whether, but of where to**.

- If your organization already runs on the Atlassian suite, JSM is the official, lowest-friction path;
- If you want to use this migration to rebuild an independent, durable on-call practice — licenses billed per responder, native China IM collaboration, built-in status pages and AI capabilities — Flashduty is worth a full evaluation.

We recommend a 14-day trial with a real workload: connect one alert source, configure schedules and escalation policies, run the full notify-and-close loop, and then make your decision.
